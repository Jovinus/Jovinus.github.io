AI researcher in physiological and clinical time-series learning

I develop and evaluate machine learning models for physiological signals and longitudinal clinical data, with particular attention to external validation and to how models behave under continuous clinical monitoring. I’m a PhD student in Digital Health at SAIHST, Sungkyunkwan University, advised by Professor Byung-Jae Lee, and an AI researcher at AITRICS.

My work began with biosignals — EMG and ECG-derived RR intervals — and moved to electronic health records and repeated clinical measurements. The question that connects them: when data arrive as a sequence, how should a model learn from it, and how should the model be evaluated in the conditions where it will be used — at other hospitals, and read repeatedly as new measurements arrive rather than once per patient?

Research Themes

  • Physiological signal learning Neural networks for EMG gesture classification, and deep learning on raw RR intervals for age prediction and stress detection, compared with handcrafted features.
  • Clinical prediction Asthma diagnosis from methacholine challenge tests and sepsis risk in hospitalised patients, validated on later referrals and a prospective cohort.
  • Real-world evaluation Multi-center external validation and continuous-monitoring simulation of AKI prediction models, where single-point performance and performance under repeated use can diverge.

Selected Journal Articles

Real-World Evaluation

  1. Deep learning models for acute kidney injury prediction: multi-center external validation and evaluation under simulated continuous monitoring conditions
    npj Digital Medicine, 2026 Co-first author
    Question
    A deployed AKI model re-predicts as new data arrive, yet models are usually compared on one prediction per patient. Does that single-point performance describe how a model behaves under continuous monitoring, and does it hold at other hospitals?
    Method
    Three deep learning architectures (LSTM-Attention, Masked CNN, ITE-Transformer) and two baselines (XGBoost, logistic regression) at 0-, 48-, and 72-hour horizons, evaluated with an online simulation that issues predictions every 12 hours before onset.
    Validation
    157,323 admissions; developed at NHIS Ilsan Hospital and externally validated at Chuncheon Sacred Heart Hospital and on MIMIC-IV. The model with the highest single-point AUROC had the heaviest alert burden under simulation.
    My role
    Conceptualization, methodology, software, data curation, formal analysis, validation, and the original draft.
    doi:10.1038/s41746-026-02722-2

Clinical Prediction

  1. Validation of an artificial intelligence-based algorithm for predictive performance and risk stratification of sepsis using real-world data from hospitalised patients: a prospective observational study
    BMJ Health & Care Informatics, 2025 Co-first author
    Question
    Does a deep learning sepsis score derived from electronic medical records hold up prospectively against conventional scoring systems?
    Method
    Prospective comparison of a deep learning sepsis algorithm (VC-SEPS) with traditional scores, including risk stratification at admission and lead time before an operational sepsis definition was met.
    Validation
    Prospective observational cohort of 6,455 hospitalised patients (325 with sepsis) at Keimyung University Dongsan Hospital.
    doi:10.1136/bmjhci-2024-101353
  2. Novel artificial intelligence-based technology to diagnose asthma using methacholine challenge tests
    Allergy, Asthma & Immunology Research, 2024 Co-first author
    Question
    Can the full spirometric response recorded during a methacholine challenge test diagnose asthma more accurately than the conventional PC20 ≤ 16 mg/mL criterion?
    Method
    Five machine learning models (logistic regression, SVM, random forest, XGBoost, neural network) using FEV1, FVC, and FEF25–75 measured across the test, compared against the PC20 criterion.
    Validation
    1,501 patients at Samsung Medical Center (2015–2020), split by referral date into a training set and a later test set; compared on AUROC and AUPRC.
    My role
    Cohort definition with clinicians, data extraction from the clinical data warehouse, model development, and Shapley-value review with clinicians.
    doi:10.4168/aair.2024.16.1.42 · Code

Physiological Signal Learning

  1. Deep learning-based stress detection from RR intervals in major depressive disorder, panic disorder, and healthy individuals
    Frontiers in Psychiatry, 2025 Co-first author
    Question
    Can acute cognitive stress be detected directly from ultra-short raw RR-interval sequences, without handcrafted heart rate variability features?
    Method
    A one-dimensional ResNet-34 trained on raw RR intervals in 5-minute and 1-minute windows, comparing group-specific with pooled training.
    Validation
    147 adults (major depressive disorder, panic disorder, healthy controls) with up to five visits over 12 weeks, each with a resting baseline and a mental-arithmetic stressor.
    My role
    Data curation, methodology, software, formal analysis, validation, visualization, and writing.
    doi:10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1672260 · Code
  2. Age prediction in healthy subjects using RR intervals and heart rate variability: A pilot study based on deep learning
    Applied Sciences, 2023 First author
    Question
    Can age group be predicted from raw RR intervals with deep learning, compared with conventional heart rate variability features?
    Method
    A modified ResNet classifying four age groups from RR intervals, benchmarked against an HRV-feature model and a combined RR + HRV model, with adaptive synthetic sampling and a hybrid classification/regression loss.
    Validation
    RR-interval recordings from 1,093 healthy subjects. The RR model performed comparably to the HRV and combined models, but no model predicted every age group reliably.
    My role
    RR-interval data preparation, model development, and analysis.
    doi:10.3390/app13052932 · Code
  3. Electromyogram-based classification of hand and finger gestures using artificial neural networks
    Sensors, 2022 Co-first author
    Question
    Can ten hand and finger gestures, including seven individual-finger gestures, be classified from a small fixed set of EMG electrodes?
    Method
    Six time-domain features from each of three EMG channels, used to train personalized classifiers — artificial neural network, SVM, random forest, and logistic regression.
    Validation
    Ten healthy subjects performing ten gestures; accuracy and inter-subject variance compared across classifiers with ANOVA and F-tests.
    My role
    Conceptualization, methodology, formal analysis, visualization, and writing, shared with the co-authors.
    doi:10.3390/s22010225 · Code
